# J1 - Hoffman (1970): On eigenvalues and colorings of graphs

- Metadata (web-verified 2026-10-13): A. J. Hoffman, "On eigenvalues and colorings of graphs", in B. Harris (ed.), Graph Theory and its Applications, Academic Press, New York (1970), pp. 79-91. Sources: World Scientific reprint chapter page, DOI 10.1142/9789812796936_0041; the companion "On eigenvalues and colorings of graphs, II", Annals New York Acad. Sci. (1970), whose introduction cites Part I in the Advanced Seminar proceedings (Wiley DOI 10.1111/j.1749-6632.1970.tb56474.x).
- Summary (general-knowledge level, flagged sketch): the founding spectral chromatic bound: the chromatic number of a graph is at least 1 + lambda_max/|lambda_min| (for d-regular graphs: chi >= 1 + d/|lambda_n|), with the associated independence-number bound for d-regular graphs, alpha <= n(-lambda_n)/(d - lambda_n), now universally called the Hoffman bound. The paper proves these bounds and surveys their applications to coloring-type questions.
- Decisive moves: (1) [P21 HIT] the spectral engine in its founding form: adjacency eigenvalues act as averaging devices - the least eigenvalue forces the chromatic number up; the argument runs through Rayleigh-type quotient estimates, exactly the Atlas P21 definition ("eigenvalues as averaging devices; operator norm on 1-perp"); (2) [P15 secondary] the target is chromatic number - the spectral bound enters the coloring toolkit as the classical Hoffman bound. Lineage note: the Atlas Ch. 8 Lecture 8 (the Hoffman bound with two proofs and machine-verified censuses) descends from this paper; J2 (theta) and J3 (Alon-Milman) sit in the spectral program it opens.
