# E11 - Galvin (1995): The list chromatic index of a bipartite multigraph

- Summary (general-knowledge level, flagged sketch): the list chromatic index of a bipartite multigraph equals its edge-chromatic number (Delta, by Konig's edge-coloring theorem): every k-edge-colourable bipartite multigraph is k-edge-choosable. The proof turns the proper Delta-edge-coloring into preference orders on the edges, builds an auxiliary digraph, and applies the kernel-painting lemma of Erdos-Rubin-Taylor (a kernel-perfect orientation with lists of size outdegree + 1 is choosable); stable matchings supply the kernels.
- Decisive moves: (1) [P25] reduction to the kernel language - list coloring translated into kernel-perfect orientations, where the ERT painting lemma carries the proof; (2) [P17] min-max identity - list chromatic index = chromatic index = Delta for bipartite multigraphs, with the Konig theorem as the base and the kernel lemma as the lift; (3) [P8] the bipartite structure is what makes the orientation kernel-perfect (stable matching existence via the two shores); (4) cross-domain note: the stable-matching bridge (Gale-Shapely lineage) is a named reduction in the field.
