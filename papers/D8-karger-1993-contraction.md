- Summary (general-knowledge level, flagged sketch): the random contraction algorithm - repeatedly contract a uniformly random edge until two vertices remain; any fixed min cut survives with probability at least 2/(n(n-1)), so about n^2 independent repetitions find the global min cut with high probability: the first strongly polynomial algorithm breaking the max-flow barrier for global min cuts, with an RNC implementation.
- Decisive moves: (1) [P12] random construction - a random process whose per-run success probability is analyzed exactly (product over phases) and amplified by repetition; (2) [P10] the counting flip - the survival bound yields that a graph has at most n(n-1)/2 min cuts, expectation/counting used in reverse; (3) [P17] min-cut found without any max-flow call - the min-max pairing broken open by randomness; (4) lineage: Karger-Stein (STOC 1993, 757-765) recursive amplification; the STOC 1994 sampling paper for cuts, flows, and network design.
