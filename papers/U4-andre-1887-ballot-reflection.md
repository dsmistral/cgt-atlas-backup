- Summary (flagged sketch, the open English translation and the ballot-theorem literature): the ballot problem's direct solution. Bertrand's problem (his own 1887 note, C. R. 105, 369, uncorpus'd): candidates A and B receive alpha and beta votes with alpha > beta; find the probability that A stays strictly ahead throughout the count. Bertrand sketched a recursion-based proof and asked for a more direct method; Andre supplies it: the FAVOURABLE sequences are counted through their complement - the unfavourable sequences split into two equally numerous cases (B receives the first vote: counted directly; A receives the first vote but the count reaches a first tie: matched to the first case by an explicit bijection exchanging the initial segment up to the first tie). The ballot theorem (alpha - beta)/(alpha + beta) follows. The modern name "Andre's reflection principle/method" - the geometric version reflecting the path at its first contact with the tie line - is a LATER repackaging: the historical-methods literature (Renault's "Lost (and Found) in Translation: Andre's Actual Method") documents that Andre's own note uses the first-tie swap, not a geometric reflection; the reflection form enters the textbook canon through later authors (Bauer's probability text; Papoulis; Comtet's careful "essentially due to Andre").
