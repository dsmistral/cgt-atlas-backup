# D7 - Karp (1972): Reducibility among combinatorial problems

- Metadata (web-verified 2026-10-13): R. M. Karp, "Reducibility Among Combinatorial Problems", in: R. E. Miller, J. W. Thatcher (eds.), Complexity of Computer Computations, Plenum Press, New York, 1972, pp. 85-103. Sources: Wikipedia (Richard M. Karp; Karp's 21 NP-complete problems; the German Wikipedia listing carrying the Plenum citation).
- Summary (general-knowledge level, flagged sketch): 21 combinatorial and graph-theoretic problems proved NP-complete by polynomial many-one reductions from Cook's SAT theorem; the paper establishes the reduction-tree methodology that has organized the field ever since.
- Decisive moves: (1) [P25] reduction/encoding as the whole engine - each problem is translated into another's language, and the encoding itself carries the hardness (the Atlas P25 description names exactly this use); (2) [P4] the reductions compose - transitivity is the induction that spreads completeness along the tree; (3) [P18] the algorithmic flip side - each NP-membership claim is a certificate-verification procedure.
