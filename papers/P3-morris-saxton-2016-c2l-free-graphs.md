# P3 - Morris-Saxton (2016): The number of C_{2l}-free graphs

- Metadata (web-verified 2026-10-13): Robert Morris, David Saxton, "The number of C_{2l}-free graphs", Advances in Mathematics 298 (2016) 534-580. DOI 10.1016/j.aim.2016.05.001. Sources: Wiley Online Library citing article (the full citation: "Advances in Mathematics 298 (2016): 534-580. 10.1016/j.aim.2016.05.001"); ScienceDirect citing-article record (volume 298, 2016); arXiv:1309.2927 (abstract; v3, 11 Nov 2015).
- Summary (flagged sketch, abstract + open-arXiv level): the number of C_{2l}-free graphs on n vertices is at most 2^{O(n^{1+1/l})}, confirming Erdos's conjecture for even cycles - the bipartite family the 1986 Erdos-Frankl-Rodl theorem does not cover. Engines, the abstract's own framing: the hypergraph container method (B7/B8) "together with a new 'balanced supersaturation theorem' for even cycles". The paper's own history section: the first breakthrough was Kleitman-Winston (P1)'s C4 case - at most 2^{(1+c)ex(n,C4)} graphs with c ~= 1.17, "within striking distance" of Erdos's sharp 2^{(1+o(1))ex} form; the paper's own discussion is UNCERTAIN whether that sharp form holds for C4 and the other complete bipartite graphs.
- Decisive moves: (1) [CAND-B5] the container method as the headline engine - the abstract's own framing; the B7/B8 theorems applied with a bespoke supersaturation input; (2) [P10] the balanced supersaturation theorem for even cycles - supersaturation refined to a roughly-uniformly-spread subcollection of copies (no edge in too many members), the input form the container algorithm needs; an averaging/distribution engine (genuine; the H5 precedent - batch H's supersaturation firing, "the Erdos-Stone engine is an averaging engine at heart"); (3) [honest note, no P-ID] the Bondy-Simonovits extremal bound ex(n,C_{2l}) = O_l(n^{1+1/l}) is the order the count matches (the later literature's summary: "settling a classical conjecture of Erdos... generalising a previous result of Kleitman and Winston, and complementing the classical Bondy-Simonovits bound").
