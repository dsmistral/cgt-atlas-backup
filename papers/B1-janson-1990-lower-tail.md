# B1 - Janson (1990): Poisson approximation for large deviations

- Metadata (web-verified 2026-10-13): S. Janson, Random Structures and Algorithms 1(2): 221-229, 1990, DOI 10.1002/rsa.3240010209. Sources: Wiley listing; MathSciNet MR1138428; Semantic Scholar. Janson's own 2016 follow-up (arXiv:1406.1248) confirms the citation.
- Summary (publisher abstract, quoted-level): upper and lower bounds for P(S <= k), 0 <= k <= ES, where S is a sum of indicator variables with the special dependency structure appearing in subgraph counts in random graphs; in typical cases the bounds are close to the corresponding Poisson probabilities with the same mean. The 2016 follow-up shows the inequality is optimal for large deviations when dependencies are weak.
- Decisive moves: (1) [P28] concentration - the Janson inequality is a lower-tail concentration tool for sums of locally-dependent indicators, one of the standard batteries alongside Azuma and Talagrand; (2) near-independence exploitation - the Delta/delta bookkeeping of dependency terms is cousin to the local-lemma regime [CAND-A1].
