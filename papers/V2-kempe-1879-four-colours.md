- Summary (flagged sketch, the Wikisource scan and the four-colour histories): the "proof" that held for eleven years. Kempe argues by minimal counterexample: a smallest map needing five colours cannot exist. Every planar map has a region touching at most five others; remove that region, four-colour the rest (induction), restore, and repair the colouring by the CHAIN ARGUMENT: if the restored region's neighbours use all four colours, interchange two colours along the connected two-colour chain containing one neighbour - if the chain does not reach the other same-coloured neighbour, the interchange frees the colour; the degree-5 case deploys the chains twice, for two different colour pairs. The argument's central device - the two-colour interchange along a connected chain (the "Kempe chain") - is REAL and survives into every later proof; the flaw is elsewhere: in the degree-5 case the TWO chain interchanges can interfere through each other (the second swap may undo the first across their shared region) - the error Heawood found in 1890 (V4).
