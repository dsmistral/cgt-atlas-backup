# A4 - Erdos (1959): Graph theory and probability

- Metadata (web-verified 2026-10-13): P. Erdos, Canad. J. Math. 11: 34-38, 1959, DOI 10.4153/CJM-1959-003-9. Sources: Cambridge Core listing (Volume 11, 1959); Wikipedia girth article (method summary). CORRECTION: the seed row said 1961; the girth/chromatic classic is the 1959 paper. The sequel Graph Theory and Probability II is Canad. J. Math. 13 (1961): 346-352.
- Summary (Wikipedia-documented method, flagged sketch): probabilistic proof that graphs of arbitrarily high girth and arbitrarily high chromatic number exist: a random graph on n vertices with edge probability ~ n^{-(g-1)/g} has at most about n/2 cycles of length at most g (with probability tending to 1) but no independent set of size n/2k; deleting one vertex from each short cycle leaves a graph with girth > g and chromatic number at least k.
- Decisive moves: (1) [P12] random construction; (2) [P10] expected-count control of short cycles; (3) [P14] alteration - delete one vertex per short cycle, the debris is affordable because the cycles are few; (4) [P10] the independence-number bound then forces high chromatic number.
