# N3 - Jain-Sah-Sawhney: anticoncentration versus the number of subset sums

- Metadata: Vishesh Jain, Ashwin Sah, Mehtaab Sawhney, "Anticoncentration versus the Number of Subset Sums", Advances in Combinatorics 2021:6, 10 pp., DOI 10.19086/aic.24872; received 19 January 2021, published 28 June 2021; arXiv:2101.07726 (v1 19 Jan 2021; v2 14 Jun 2021). - Abstract or faithful summary (summarized, flagged sketch): for w in R^n, if some subset sum is popular - #{xi in {0,1}^n : <xi,w> = tau} >= 2^{-epsilon n} * 2^n - then the number of DISTINCT subset sums is at most 2^{O(sqrt(epsilon) n)} (Theorem 1.2: |R(w)| <= exp(C sqrt(epsilon) n)), for any n^{-2} <= epsilon <= 1. This exponentially improves the epsilon-dependence of Nederlof-Pawlewicz-Swennenhuis-Wegrzycki (delta(epsilon) from O(log log(1/epsilon) / sqrt(log(1/epsilon))) to O(sqrt(epsilon))), with the same black-box improvement for parameterized bin packing; the authors conjecture the true dependence is linear and give an example showing sqrt(epsilon) is needed up to constants. The editorial introduction positions the paper in the inverse-Littlewood-Offord theme: the Tao-Vu structural route (generalized arithmetic progressions) is hopeless at popularity 2^{-epsilon n} (too many exceptional elements, per the paper's own intro), so the conclusion is the weaker arithmetic datum - the COUNT of distinct sums.
