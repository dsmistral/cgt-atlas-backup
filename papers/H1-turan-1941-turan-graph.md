# H1 - Turan (1941): Eine Extremalaufgabe aus der Graphentheorie

- Metadata (web-verified 2026-10-13): P. Turan, "Eine Extremalaufgabe aus der Graphentheorie", Matematikai es Fizikai Lapok 48: 436-452 (1941) (in Hungarian, with German summary). Sources: SpringerLink reference lists (three independent articles citing Mat. Fiz. Lapok 48 (1941), 436-452), ScienceDirect JCT reference, Bondy-Murty chapter notes. Print Hungarian journal; no DOI.
- Summary (general-knowledge level, flagged sketch): the founding paper of extremal graph theory: the maximum number of edges of an n-vertex graph with no K_{r+1} is attained uniquely by the Turan graph T_r(n), the complete balanced r-partite graph. The paper introduces the Turan graphs and the exact extremal value.
- Decisive moves: (1) [P8] extremal optimization: analyze a K_{r+1}-free graph with the maximum number of edges and force its structure; (2) [P4] Turan's original proof runs by induction on n with vertex deletion and case analysis; (3) [P2 partial] the slick modern proof - averaging over the non-neighbors of a maximum clique, with an edge-counting finish - is retrospective exposition rather than the paper's own engine; expected P2 is recorded as partial, P8 as HIT. Batch-lineage note: this theorem is the foundation on which G2 (Erdos-Stone), G3 (limit theorem) and G4 (stability) were built.
