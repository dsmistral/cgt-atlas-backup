# N2 - Sah-Sawhney-Stoner-Zhao: Kahn's conjecture settled by three undergraduates and their mentor

- Metadata: Ashwin Sah, Mehtaab Sawhney, David Stoner, Yufei Zhao, "The number of independent sets in an irregular graph", J. Combin. Theory Ser. B 138: 172-195 (2019); arXiv:1805.04021 (v1 10 May 2018; v3 28 Jan 2019). Verified via the MIT DSpace citation record (vol. 138, 2019, pp. 172-195), the open par.nsf.gov/DSpace PDF, the MIT News feature (Feb 25, 2019), and the open expository reproduction of the proof (Chheda-Goel-Qiao, "Number of Independent Sets in Regular and Irregular Graphs: A 31 Year Journey", arXiv:2405.18815). AUTHOR-LEVEL NOTE (honest): three undergraduates at the time (Sah - MIT sophomore; Sawhney - MIT junior; Stoner - Harvard senior) plus their faculty mentor Yufei Zhao (MIT assistant professor); the load-bearing technical work is credited to the students by the MIT News record - the batch's "youth-authored" claim is recorded with the mentor coauthor noted, never silently.
- Abstract or faithful summary (summarized, flagged sketch): settles Kahn's 2001 conjecture. Main theorem: for a graph G without isolated vertices, i(G) <= prod_{uv in E(G)} i(K_{d_u,d_v})^{1/(d_u d_v)}, with equality iff G is a disjoint union of complete bipartite graphs; the hard-core partition-function (weighted) version is also proved. History per the paper and the expository reproduction: Granville's 1988 Banff conjecture -> Alon 1991 -> Kahn 2001 (bipartite d-regular, the entropy argument; the P29 flagship lineage) -> Zhao 2009/2010 (all d-regular, the bipartite-swapping trick; Galvin-Zhao computer-assisted for maximum degree <= 5; the recent occupancy method does not extend) -> this paper (all graphs without isolated vertices).
- The decisive moves (three-step structure per the expository reproduction):
  1. [P25 genuine - expected by seed] Zhao's bipartite-swapping reduction is step 1: the double cover G x K2 satisfies i(G)^2 <= i(G x K2) (an explicit injection of pairs of independent sets) and j(G)^2 = j(G x K2) (where j is the conjectured upper-bound expression), reducing the general theorem to the bipartite case.
  2. [P4 genuine - unplanned by seed] induction on the number of vertices with the independent-set recursion i(G) = i(G-w) + i(G-w-N(w)) at a maximum-degree vertex w - the freshman-level recursion IS the proof's skeleton, reducing the theorem to the local inequality j(G-w) + j(G-w-N(w)) <= j(G).
  3. [P10 partial - expected by seed; the P29 story honestly inverted] the local inequality is proved by HOLDER'S INEQUALITY plus computation (bookkeeping layered by distance from w). Kahn's entropy argument - the conjecture's origin - does NOT extend to irregular graphs (Shearer's inequality cannot be applied losslessly, per the paper's own discussion); the proof AVOIDS entropy. Honest verdict on the seed's P29 flagship expectation: P29 MISSED as the in-proof engine (lineage note: per Lubetzky-Zhao, a Holder one-liner reinterprets Kahn's entropy proof for the regular case - the convexity face stands in for entropy here, credited under P10).
  4. [P9 MISSED - expected by seed] no pigeonhole/double-counting move is load-bearing; the swap's counting content is the injection recorded under P25.
- Accessibility datum (the batch's research question): the proof's SKELETON is genuinely accessible - the independent-set recursion and the double-cover swap are undergraduate-level moves - but the technical verification (the layered Holder inequality) is heavy; MIT News quotes Sawhney: "The approach in the paper, while not very difficult, ends up being very technical", and Zhao: "Lots of experienced mathematicians have worked on this conjecture without success." The colorings/homomorphisms analogue conjectured in the paper was settled by the authors in follow-up work (not re-pinned here).
