- Summary (flagged sketch, the zero-error literature and the corpus's J2 record): the zero-error information theory's origin. Shannon defines the zero-error capacity C0 (the least upper bound of transmissible rates with no decoding errors), models the channel by its confusability graph, and turns the coding problem graph-theoretic: a zero-error code is an independent set in the graph, block codes live in the strong power, and the capacity is the asymptotic independence rate - the encoding IS the theory. Results: the feedback theorem (the zero-error capacity with feedback may exceed the no-feedback value and is solved - the abstract record's own statement; the ordinary capacity's feedback equivalence the contrast), and the pentagon: five pairwise nonconfusable two-letter words for C5 (the sqrt(5) achievability), with the matching upper bound left open - the pentagon capacity problem, resolved 23 years later by the corpus's J2 (Lovasz 1979: theta(C5) = sqrt(5)).
