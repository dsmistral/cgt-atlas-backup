# D9 - Christofides (1976) / Serdyukov (1978): Worst-case analysis of a new heuristic for the travelling salesman problem

- Metadata (web-verified 2026-10-13): N. Christofides, "Worst-case analysis of a new heuristic for the travelling salesman problem", Technical Report 388, Graduate School of Industrial Administration, Carnegie-Mellon University (1976) - never journal-published. Sources: BibSonomy record; DTIC (ADA025602); Semantic Scholar. A. I. Serdyukov discovered the algorithm independently in 1976 and published in 1978 (Soviet journal literature). Sources: Wikipedia "Christofides algorithm" (incl. Serdyukov's stated O(n^3 log n) complexity, a less efficient matching algorithm being available to him); the "historical note" paper on the 3/2-algorithm (ResearchGate). The attribution "Christofides-Serdyukov" is now standard. The seed's "(verify)" on venues is resolved.
- Summary (general-knowledge level, flagged sketch): metric TSP approximation with ratio 3/2: compute a minimum spanning tree T; the odd-degree vertices of T form a min-weight perfect matching M; T + M is connected and Eulerian; shortcut an Euler tour to a Hamiltonian cycle (triangle inequality makes shortcutting lossless); cost(T) + cost(M) is at most (3/2) OPT.
- Decisive moves: (1) [P25] reduction/encoding - the TSP instance is encoded into the MST + matching structure, with the triangle inequality exactly what makes the encoding lossless; (2) [P7] handshaking parity - the odd-degree set has even cardinality, so the perfect matching exists at all; (3) [P10] averaging inside the cost bound - the optimal tour restricted to the odd vertices splits into two perfect matchings, so one costs at most OPT/2; (4) [P18] the heuristic with a worst-case proof - the founding instance of approximation-ratio analysis; (5) [P8]-flavored: cost(T) <= OPT because deleting a tour edge leaves a spanning tree.
