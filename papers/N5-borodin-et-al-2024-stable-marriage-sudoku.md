- Abstract (faithfully summarized from the arXiv preprint; the CMJ abstract not fetchable through Cloudflare): the paper translates the stable marriage problem into the language of Sudoku and back - preference profiles drawn in Sudoku grids, blocking pairs and the Gale-Shapley algorithm in Sudoku language, mutually Latin / disjoint / joint profiles, the new joint-groups Sudoku invented with two puzzles; the case n = 2 completely analyzed.
- The decisive moves (flagged sketch; verified against the open arXiv HTML):
  1. The bidirectional SMP <-> Sudoku encoding: preference profiles in Sudoku grids (Section 3); disjoint-groups and joint-groups Sudokus with their profile types (Sections 4-6) - the whole-paper move. [P25]
  2. Unique stable matching under shared preferences (Proposition 1): the most-desirable pair are soulmates, forced in every stable matching; remove them and recurse - an induction on n. [P4]
  3. The key function is a bijection (Proposition 2): if f(i1) = f(i2) = j then men are ranked j at least 2n times, so some man is ranked j by two women - a contradiction; a pigeonhole argument. [P9]
  4. Joint profiles are mutually Latin (Theorem 3): the key bijection's inverse is the men's key function; both preference matrices are Latin squares - the bijective correspondence (a joint profile is uniquely defined by its key function) whose counting content lands in the companion paper N6. [P1]
  5. Joint-groups Sudoku introduced and classified for n = 3 up to symmetries; existence plus two puzzles (Sections 6-8). [P25]
