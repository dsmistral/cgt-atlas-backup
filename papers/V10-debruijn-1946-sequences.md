- Summary (flagged sketch, the TU/e record, the Wikipedia de-Bruijn-sequence history, and the universal-cycles literature): the de Bruijn sequence's origin paper. The problem: cyclic binary sequences of period 2^n in which every length-n word occurs exactly once ("maximal cyclic sequences"); de Bruijn proves existence and derives the count 2^(2^(n-1)-n) (the universal-cycles literature's record: "de Bruijn showed that there are 2^(2^(n-1)-n) such sequences... in 1946, and (k!)^(k^(n-1)-n) for T(n,k) in joint" work). Per de Bruijn's own later account: existence for binary alphabets was first proved by Camille Flye Sainte-Marie in 1894 (answering a question of A. de Riviere, with the same count), a result "largely forgotten"; the k-letter generalization is the joint van Aardenne-Ehrenfest-de Bruijn work (1951). The modern face - the shift-register state diagram (the de Bruijn graph) with its Euler circuits - is the retrospective repackaging (uncorpus'd lineage).
