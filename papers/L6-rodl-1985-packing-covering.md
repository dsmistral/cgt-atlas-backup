# L6 - Rodl's nibble: the Erdos-Hanani conjecture

- Abstract or faithful summary (summarized, flagged sketch): proves the Erdos-Hanani conjecture (Erdos-Hanani, "On a limit theorem in combinatorial analysis", Publ. Math. Debrecen 10: 10-13 (1963), pinned via citing bibliographies): for fixed k and t, the maximum packing of t-subsets into k-subsets (equivalently the minimum covering) is asymptotically the counting bound C(v,t)/C(k,t) as v -> infinity - one can get asymptotically close to a perfect tactical configuration (Erdos and Hanani had settled l=2 and some l=3 cases). The engine is the SEMI-RANDOM NIBBLE, born here: build the packing in many small random greedy steps, at each step choosing a few k-sets nearly uniformly among those disjoint from earlier choices, and track the quasi-uniformity of the residual t-set graph so every "nibble" consumes nearly its expected share; the iteration leaves an o(1) fraction of t-sets uncovered. The method's descendants: the hypergraph matching/coloring nibble (Pippenger-Spencer, L9) and ultimately the design-existence era (Keevash, L8).
