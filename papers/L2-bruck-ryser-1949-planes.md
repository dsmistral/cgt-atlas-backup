# L2 - Bruck-Ryser: the nonexistence of certain finite projective planes

- Metadata: R. H. Bruck and H. J. Ryser, "The nonexistence of certain finite projective planes", Canadian Journal of Mathematics 1(1): 88-93 (February 1949), DOI 10.4153/CJM-1949-009-2. Verified via the Cambridge Core article page (Volume 1, Issue 1, 01 February 1949) and the Bruck-Ryser-Chowla theorem Wikipedia reference; a German Wikipedia "88-92" page-range variant recorded as a citation error. AUTHORSHIP corrected: the 1949 paper is Bruck-Ryser only; Sarvadaman Chowla's part of BRC is the 1950 generalization to symmetric block designs (Ryser-Chowla) - the theorem's name compresses the 1949 plane case and the 1950 design case.
- Abstract or faithful summary (summarized, flagged sketch): the Bruck-Ryser theorem: if a finite projective plane of order n exists and n is congruent to 1 or 2 (mod 4), then n is a sum of two squares. Consequences: no plane of order 6 or 14 (and 21, 22, 30, ...); orders 10 and 12 are NOT excluded (order 10 fell later to the Lam-Swiercz-Thiel computer search). For symmetric designs (with Chowla 1950): v even forces k - lambda to be a perfect square; v odd forces the Diophantine equation x^2 = (k - lambda)y^2 + (-1)^((v-1)/2) lambda z^2 to have a nonzero integer solution.
- The decisive moves:
  1. [P19] The incidence-matrix identity: encode the plane as its incidence matrix N; the Gram identity NN^T = nI + J plus the determinant / quadratic-form integrality argument on the matrix is the engine of the theorem.
  2. [P41] Arithmetic nonexistence: the integrality/solvability punchline (sum of two squares; quadratic-form solvability) forbids existence before any search - the birth-certificate move in its origin paper.
  3. [P2 partial] The incidence identity itself is double counting (point pairs on lines counted two ways) - the arithmetic root under the P19 engine.
- New-pattern candidates: none. (The Hasse-Minkowski connection - the conditions are sufficient for a rational incidence matrix - is recorded as lineage, not a corpus pattern.)
