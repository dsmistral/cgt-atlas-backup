# L3 - Hanani: on some tactical configurations

- Metadata: Haim Hanani, "On some tactical configurations", Canadian Journal of Mathematics 15: 702-722 (1963), DOI 10.4153/CJM-1963-069-5. Verified via the Cambridge Core article page (Volume 15, 1963; the abstract carries the C[k, l, lambda, v] definition) and the Haim Hanani Wikipedia publication list; the Utah CJM table-of-contents listing documents the seed's conflation source (Hanani's "On covering of balanced incomplete block designs", CJM 12 (1960), and "On quadruple systems" in an earlier volume).
- Abstract or faithful summary (partly quoted from the Cambridge Core abstract, flagged sketch): "Given a set E of v elements, and given positive integers k, l (l <= k <= v), and lambda, we understand by a tactical configuration C[k, l, lambda, v] a system of subsets of E, having k elements each, such that every subset of E having l elements is contained in exactly lambda sets of the system. A necessary condition for the existence of a configuration C[k, l, lambda, v] is known..." The paper proves the necessary (divisibility) conditions are SUFFICIENT for the families C[3,2,1] (Steiner triple systems), C[4,2,1], and C[4,3,1] (Steiner quadruple systems): each exists exactly for its admissible v - the SQS existence theorem (v = 2 or 4 mod 6).
- The decisive moves:
  1. [P4] Recursive construction: designs built from smaller designs via pairwise-balanced decompositions and fill-in ("hole") constructions - the whole existence proof is an induction on v.
  2. [P11] Explicit base-case constructions (difference-type placements) feeding the recursion.
  3. [P41] The admissibility arithmetic is the exact theorem statement here: divisibility necessary AND sufficient for these families - the converse direction of the birth certificate.
