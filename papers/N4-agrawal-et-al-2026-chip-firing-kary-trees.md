- Abstract (quoted from the journal PDF): "We use an infinite k-ary tree with a self-loop at the root as our underlying graph. We consider a chip-firing process starting with N chips at the root. We describe the stable configurations. We calculate the number of fires for each vertex and the total number of fires. We study a sequence of the number of root fires for a given k as a function of N and study its properties. We do the same for the total number of fires."
- The decisive moves (flagged sketch; verified against the open journal PDF):
  1. Stable configurations by induction on n: with (k^n-1)/(k-1) <= N <= (k^(n+1)-k)/(k-1), the chips form a perfect k-ary tree of height n-1 with equal chip counts per layer (Proposition 3.1; the parallel firing strategy + the subtree-isomorphism reduction + the global-confluence tool). [P4]
  2. Base-k digit encoding: chips per layer = 1 + the digits of the base-k expansion of N - (k^n-1)/(k-1) (Proposition 3.2) - the representation that carries the fire-count formulas. [P25]
  3. Chip-flow conservation: the difference in fires between adjacent layers = the chips at descendants / k (Lemma 4.1; chips sent to children counted both ways: resident descendants + k x child fires). [P2]
  4. Fire counts by backward induction (Theorem 4.1) and the recursive root-fire formula f0(N) = floor(N/k) - 1 + f0(floor(N/k) - 1) (Corollary 4.3); the partial-sums recurrence b(n,k) (Proposition 5.3). [P4]
