# G5 - Ruzsa-Szemeredi (1978): Triple systems with no six points carrying three triangles

- Metadata (web-verified 2026-10-13): I. Z. Ruzsa and E. Szemeredi, "Triple systems with no six points carrying three triangles", in: A. Hajnal and V. T. Sos (eds.), Combinatorics, Vol. II (Proc. Fifth Hungarian Colloq., Keszthely, 1976), Colloq. Math. Soc. Janos Bolyai 18, North-Holland, Amsterdam, 1978, pp. 939-945. - Summary (general-knowledge level, flagged sketch): the (6,3)-problem: a triple system in which no six points carry three triples has o(n^2) triples. The proof passes through an early form of the triangle-removal lemma (a graph with o(n^3) triangles can be made triangle-free by deleting o(n^2) edges), making this the BIRTHPLACE of the removal lemma (silent P31); the regularity lemma (G1) is the engine. A Behrend-based construction shows the o(n^2) bound is far from trivial, with n^2/exp(O(sqrt(log n))) triples.
- Decisive moves: (1) [P31 origin] FIRST GENUINE USAGE EVIDENCE for silent P31, in its founding instance: counting-removal - a counting statement (few configurations) is converted into a deletion statement (few edges), with the counting lemma supplied by regularity; (2) [P30] the regularity machinery as engine - the founding demonstration that P30 turns local density into counting; (3) [P11 explicit construction] the lower bound: Behrend's 3-AP-free sets are encoded into large (6,3)-systems - a number-theoretic construction transported to a combinatorial witness; the same construction pattern resurfaces decades later in property-testing lower bounds.
