# K3 - Kruskal (1963): The number of simplices in a complex

- Metadata (web-verified 2026-10-13): J. B. Kruskal, "The number of simplices in a complex", in R. Bellman (ed.), Mathematical Optimization Techniques, University of California Press, Berkeley (1963). Sources: the Kruskal-Katona Wikipedia page's citation record; the volume's table of contents (chapter 12, "The Number of Simplices in a Complex, by Joseph B. Kruskal").
- Summary (general-knowledge level, flagged sketch): the original publication of the shadow theorem: if a k-uniform family has size m, written in the cascade (binomial) representation of m, then its (k-1)-shadow has at least the corresponding cascade value - equivalently, a complete characterization of the possible f-vectors of simplicial complexes.
- Decisive moves: (1) [P24 HIT - flagship] the first genuine firing of the silent shadow-pricing pattern: price the family by its own real parameter x - a k-family of size C(x, k) forces every j-shadow to size at least C(x, j) - the move the Atlas P24 entry names after Kruskal-Katona; (2) [P23 partial] Kruskal's own argument uses transformation/normalization steps that foreshadow compression; the clean full-compression proof is retrospective (Clements-Lindstrom 1969) - the same original-vs-retrospective treatment as I1 (Sperner) and I2 (EKR); (3) lineage note: one of five independent discoveries of the theorem (see K2's record).
