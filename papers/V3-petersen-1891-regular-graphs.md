- Summary (flagged sketch, the open Project Euclid scan and the matching-theory histories): factor theory's origin. Petersen proves the factor theorem for cubic graphs: every bridgeless cubic graph has a perfect matching (a 1-factor), and studies the decomposition of regular graphs into factors more generally - every regular graph of even degree splits into 2-factors - formulating the cubic bridgeless case in connection with Tait's edge-colouring formulation of the four-colour problem. "By today's standards Petersen's proof is complicated" - the standard historical assessment (the graph-theory histories): the argument works by reductions and case analysis, carrying alternating-chain embryonics but not the augmenting-path engine (Berge, the Hungarian method - retrospective by some seventy years).
- Decisive moves: (1) [P16 partial] the factor-theorem origin: the augmentation family's counting-existence theorem (cubic bridgeless => perfect matching) proved at the family's origin - the census credits the theorem's birth, honestly NOT the augmenting-path engine (the alternating-chain embryonics are present; the modern engine is the later form, no double credit with the D-batch flagship firings); (2) [lineage] the Tait/four-colour connection (the bridgeless cubic 1-factor as the three-edge-colouring route toward the four-colour problem - the reason the theorem exists), and the descendants: Konig's theorems (the D-batch matching/edge-colouring line), Hall's theorem (V8), Tutte's 1-factor theorem (V15 - the general-graph extension), the blossom algorithm (D3, the computational descendant); (3) [lineage] the Petersen graph enters the literature here - the smallest bridgeless cubic graph with no 3-edge-colouring, the canonical counterexample of edge-colouring theory.
