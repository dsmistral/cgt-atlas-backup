### T1 full-text pass (28b, 2026-10-14) - four lecture units read and pinned against the dossiers

Provenance honesty: the OCW lecture-note PDFs are student-scribe notes by Andrew Lin for Postnikov's course (the PDFs' own headers). Scope honesty: the 28b pass covered the four highest-priority units flagged at 28a; the remaining units stay candidates until the 28c pass.

- LECTURE 5, "Proof of the hook-length formula based on a random hook walk" (February 15, 2019) - P10 PARTIAL, pinned honestly. The decisive move is a probability-distribution INTERPRETATION of the hook-length identity - the notes' own words: "We can think of this as a probability distribution! We want to construct a random process so that these are probabilities." The hook walk is engineered so its corner-absorption probabilities ARE the identity's terms (the claim p(v) = n/(H(lambda) H(lambda - v)); the walk: pick a box uniformly at random, jump within the hook with equal probability, stop at a corner); NOT a beats-the-average argument, so the P10 credit is the dossier's probabilistic-interpretation face, partial. P2 CROSS-NOTE: the absorption probability P(u, v) is a sum over all hook walks, evaluated through the rectangle additive identity for inverse hook weights ((h(a)-1) + (h(d)-1) = (h(b)-1) + (h(c)-1), collapsing at corners) - a two-way count. Aid recommendation CONFIRMED from 28a: intuition-first walkthrough (the identity read as a probability distribution before any walk is defined).
- LECTURE 17, "Sperner's property and more" (March 15, 2019) - P9 GENUINE at the instructional level. The decisive move is the symmetric-chain-decomposition pigeonhole: "any antichain cannot contain two elements in the same chain C_i. Thus the maximal size of any antichain is m" - m symmetric chains, at most one antichain element each, the middle rank wins; the V11 Dilworth P9 precedent now has its instructional mirror. Dilworth (1950) and the chain/antichain dual are stated in the same lecture; the Greene (1976) theorem appears via the lambda(P)/mu(P) conjugacy, with the increasing/decreasing-subsequence reading connecting to Greene-Schensted. SCRIBE ERROR, logged per the corrections-never-silent rule: the notes attribute the dual theorem to "Minsky, 1971" - the theorem is MIRSKY's (1971); a student-notes error, recorded here and never propagated to the census.
- LECTURE 21, "Franklin's combinatorial proof of Euler's pentagonal number theorem" (April 1, 2019) - P1's INVOLUTION FACE, pinned. The notes state the sign-reversing pairing explicitly: "Our goal is to find an involution sigma on almost all partitions of n with distinct parts, such that sigma sends a partition with an odd number of parts to an even number of parts and vice versa", and name the method: "This is related to the involution principle!" The even-vs-odd count collapses to the pentagonal numbers (the unpaired fixed shapes) - the notes' own peven_dist(n) - podd_dist(n) dichotomy. The chunk-15 C4 verdict (the sign-reversing involution a FACE of P1) has its designed instructional flagship. Aid recommendation CONFIRMED from 28a: visual scaffold (the add/remove-the-marked-part construction drawn on partition diagrams).
- LECTURE 23, "Two combinatorial proofs of Cayley's formula" (April 5, 2019) - P1 GENUINE, twice in one unit. Proof 1 (Prufer, 1918): the Prufer code as an explicit invertible map, with the bijection requirement itself the teaching point - "To show this is actually a bijection, we need to be able to invert our construction!" - and the degree-refinement proposition (a vertex appears deg(v) - 1 times in the code; the multinomial count of degree-prescribed trees) as the payoff. Proof 2: trees matched to the maps f: [n] -> [n] fixing 1 and n ("Turns out this is a bijection between all trees on n nodes and all maps f!"), the route through one-line/cycle notation. Aid recommendation CONFIRMED from 28a: worked micro-proofs - two complete bijective arguments in one lecture, with inversion made explicit; the strongest single P1 teaching unit recorded in this track.

Census consequence: these four units are TEACHING-track records only; per the track charter they record NO paper firings and touch no dossier or INDEX. The Matrix-Tree/Cauchy-Binet (P19 candidate vs coverage observation), B.E.S.T. (P25 candidate) and GF-spine (P5) units remain UNDECIDED - deferred to 28c.

### T1 full-text pass (28c, 2026-10-14) - the three deferred candidates decided

- LECTURES 26-28, "Matrix Tree Theorem" + "Matrix Tree Theorem (cont.)" + "Proof of Matrix Tree Theorem using Cauchy-Binnet formula and more" (April 2019; "Binnet" is the notes' own title spelling, logged as a scribe sic): the P19 candidate RESOLVED AS PARTIAL. The "encode sets as vectors" face fires genuinely: edge sets are encoded as columns of the reduced oriented incidence matrix, and acyclicity is detected algebraically ("we have a cycle! The column vectors ... therefore the columns are linearly dependent"), with the leaf-stripping induction closing the +-1 lemma ("Expanding out the determinant by this row ... Eventually, we get to a tree on 2 vertices, which has determinant +-1"). The "dimension bounds" face does NOT fire (no rank/dimension lower bound anywhere - the census history's engine is absent). The counting step itself - the Cauchy-Binet expansion det(B~ B~^T) = sum over S of det(B~_S)^2, "each spanning tree gives (+-1)^2 = 1, and everything else gives 0" - is honestly a COVERAGE OBSERVATION: determinant-expansion counting has no clean Atlas P-ID. Verdict: P19 PARTIAL (the vector-encoding face) + the determinant-expansion engine as a coverage observation - the 28a either/or resolved as BOTH, each honestly bounded. Aid recommendation: worked micro-proof (the Cauchy-Binet +-1 lemma is the linear-algebra family's best freshman-adjacent teaching moment).
- LECTURE 32, "Eulerian cycles in digraphs and B.E.S.T. theorem" (April 29, 2019): the P25 candidate RESOLVED AS GENUINE. The proof encodes every Eulerian cycle (root r and starting edge e fixed) by the marked-edge in-tree plus the local orderings of the remaining out-edges at each vertex - "# of Eulerian cycles in G = In_r(G) * prod over v of (outdegree(v) - 1)!" - with the in-tree count imported from the directed Matrix Tree Theorem (the notes: "we know the value of In_r(G) from the directed matrix tree theorem"; for Eulerian digraphs Lin = Lout, so all cofactors agree). P1 CROSS-NOTE: the encode/decode step is an explicit bijection (a cycle induces the total edge order <_C from which the in-tree is marked; conversely the tree + local orders reconstruct the unique cycle), but the load-bearing move is the ENCODING of the counted object into (tree, orderings) - P25's face, not a bare object-to-object match. Aid recommendation: worked micro-proof (the encode/decode walkthrough - the cleanest P25 teaching example recorded in this track).
- LECTURE 1, "Catalan numbers I" (February 2019): the P5 candidate RESOLVED AS GENUINE - the GF spine is real at the instructional level. The lecture runs the full pipeline: the first-return decomposition of Dyck paths proves the recurrence C_n = sum C_{k-1} C_{n-k}; then "we can actually write this as a generating function ... in combinatorics we avoid these questions by treating these as formal power series"; the recurrence is translated into the functional equation for f(x) and solved to f(x) = (1 - sqrt(1 - 4x))/(2x). P4 CROSS-NOTE: the recurrence engine is established (the decomposition proof) but no induction is used - the generating function does the solving - so P4 stays a cross-note here (its pure instructional firing is the 6.042J Fibonacci problem, TEACHING-CENSUS.md ps3-1). The lec21 opening (the product generating function for p(n)) corroborates the GF spine across the course.
