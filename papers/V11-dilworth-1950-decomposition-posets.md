- Summary (flagged sketch, the theorem literature): the poset decomposition theorem's origin: in a finite partially ordered set, the maximum size of an antichain equals the minimum number of chains in a chain decomposition (the width theorem). The original argument is long enough that it generated a short-proofs literature - the Israel J. Math. 1963 note ("a short proof of the following theorem is given"), the Dantzig-Hoffman 1956 linear-inequalities route, and the standard Konig/Hall-based textbook proof - the proof-history datum recorded honestly. The theorem became order theory's structural workhorse, with Mirsky's 1971 dual (min antichain cover = longest chain) and the Erdos-Szekeres corollary (rs+1 elements force a chain of r+1 or an antichain of s+1) the standard companions.
