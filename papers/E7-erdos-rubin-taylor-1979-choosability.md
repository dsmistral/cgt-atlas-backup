# E7 - Erdos-Rubin-Taylor (1979): Choosability in graphs

- Metadata (web-verified 2026-10-13): P. Erdos, A. L. Rubin, H. Taylor, "Choosability in graphs", in: Proceedings, West Coast Conference on Combinatorics, Graph Theory and Computing (Arcata, CA, Sept. 5-7, 1979), Congressus Numerantium 26 (1979): 125-157 - pages per standard citation, flagged. Sources: the ACM DL reference list of Thomassen's 5-choosability paper (venue wording); the Electronic Journal of Combinatorics citing article (content). Honest note: Vizing (1976) independently introduced list coloring (see E3) - shared attribution.
- Summary (general-knowledge level, flagged sketch): the founding paper of list coloring / choosability: it introduces the choice number, proves that choice number and chromatic number genuinely diverge (bipartite graphs with arbitrarily large choice number, via the hypergraph correspondence), characterizes the graphs that are k-choosable for small k (notably the 2-choosable graphs), and poses the two planar conjectures resolved later by Thomassen (5-choosable, E9) and Voigt (not 4-choosable, E10). It also contains the kernel-painting lemma: a kernel-perfect orientation with lists of size outdegree + 1 is choosable - the engine Galvin later exploited (E11).
- Decisive moves: (1) [P25] the correspondence - the minimum order of a complete bipartite graph that is not r-choosable equals the minimum number of edges of an r-uniform hypergraph that is not 2-colourable (a two-way reduction between list coloring and hypergraph property-B); (2) [P8] extremal characterization - the exact structure of 2-choosable graphs (block decomposition into special bipartite pieces and theta graphs); (3) [P15] induction over blocks; (4) [P11] explicit list-assignment constructions (K_{3,3} and friends) separating choice number from chromatic number.
